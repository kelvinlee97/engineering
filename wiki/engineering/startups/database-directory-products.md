A small SaaS pattern from one interview: sell access to a curated database that saves people tedious manual research. The interviewee reports a portfolio of three such products at about $32,000 per month; all figures are self-reported.[^database-websites-video]

## The sequence he describes

1. Pick a directory or database that solves a specific, painful problem, ideally one you have had yourself; his first came from manually building an investor list while fundraising.[^database-websites-video]
2. Collect the data manually at first, or automate if you can; his team assembled about 40,000 investors.[^database-websites-video]
3. Launch, get attention, and treat feedback, sign-ups, and payments as signals.[^database-websites-video]
4. Grow through content and SEO; he recommends programmatic SEO for directory products and says it brings half their revenue.[^database-websites-video]

The hosts' reading is that the model lasts because it solves a painful problem rather than following a trend, and that B2B directories may be easier to monetize than B2C, which the note labels as the host's opinion.[^database-websites-video]
